The problem

Today the street works for no one

Gachibowli and the Financial District have no rail at all; the metro's Blue Line ends at Raidurg. Hundreds of thousands of IT workers drive or ride two-wheelers on roads built only for cars.

No footpath

People walk in the traffic lane, between parked cars and moving autos.

The kerb is a car park

Cars, autos and two-wheelers fill the edge of the road.

No room for buses or scooters

Three general lanes each way and nothing else. A short trip to the office is not walkable and not safe on two wheels.

The theory: a traffic model for cities

Spine, loops, tributaries and branches

Chaos is not too many vehicles. It is every vehicle going in every direction. Order comes from a hierarchy: a spine, loops, tributaries and branches, each doing only its own job and meeting the next only at fixed points.

The principle: how the body moves blood

Arterial spine

The aorta carries the largest flow. In the city: the metro line.

Circulation loops

Blood keeps moving in loops. In the city: Metro Loops of median buses round each office hub.

Tributaries

Arteries branch off to each region. In the city: Metro Corridors joining a loop at a Metro Node.

Branches

Fine capillary branches reach every cell. In the city: walking, standing e-scooters and registered autos from home to the nearest Metro Node.

Two rules make it work: the big vessels never serve a cell directly, and layers meet only at fixed junctions.

KBR Park: a loop with tributaries in Hyderabad today

Road No. 2, Road No. 10, Road No. 92 and Road No. 1 ring KBR Park in a loop of about 5 km. About eight main roads join it at fixed junctions, so trips go round the park, not through it, and the metro spine touches the loop at Jubilee Hills Check Post.

The lesson is the shape. KBR still jams at peak because every vehicle shares it; Cyberabad's Metro Loops give the median lane to buses only.

Dubai: a city built along one spine

The spine

Red Line on Sheikh Zayed Road: 15 stations in about 26 km, one every 1.9 km.

A tram loop

Through Dubai Marina and JBR, joined to the metro at DMCC and Sobha Realty.

A Downtown loop

A boulevard round Burj Khalifa, with a covered walkway to the station.

Feeders and bridges

Buses from stations into neighbourhoods; footbridges so nobody crosses the road on foot.

20 private rides at ₹50–100, or 14 shared runs at ₹25 a seat, from a stand at the node.

Autos serve the branches, not the arteries

  • Dedicated stands at every node, never on the walkway or scooter lane
  • Every auto registered to a zone and tracked by GPS
  • No stopping or plying along Metro Loop and Corridor roads, phased in with drivers' unions
  • The metro gets a consistent, controlled flow of people

Key benefits

  • Serves working people and auto drivers sustainably
  • Reliable, comfortable transport
  • Cleaner air: no dust pollution, no noise pollution
  • Healthier people who walk and scoot every day
  • Less chaos: smooth, controlled movement

A private auto every day (₹50–100 a trip) would cost ₹2,200–4,400 a month on its own, so the everyday last mile is walking, a scooter or a shared auto. All figures approximate.

The idea

Walk, scoot or auto to the bus. Ride the bus to work.

Four parts that only work together. The bus carries the long part of the trip; the scooter and the walkway cover the first and last kilometre; residential streets make the trip from the gate safe; and a simple fare makes changing buses painless.

01

Bus in the median

Three one-way loops and three two-way corridors, a bus every 5–8 minutes at peak.

02

Walk-and-scoot edge

A 1.5 m walkway, 2.0 m scooter lane and planted kerb on every bus road.

03

Home-to-station streets

Slow, safe residential streets within 3–5 km of every station.

04

One simple fare

₹10–20 on the bus, ₹50–100 by auto; one tap with the metro card.

The bus network

Seven routes, each checked on the map

Every route was checked on OpenStreetMap. Where there is a separate road back, buses run one way round a loop and need only one median lane. Where there is only one main road, as from Miyapur, KPHB and Chandanagar, buses run both ways on a corridor. Loops that share a road run the same way along it, like meshed gears.

Why loops: one lane fits an 80-ft road

A normal median busway needs a lane each way plus a central platform, about 7.6 to 10.6 m, and buses with right-side doors.

Because each loop road only ever carries buses one way, one 4 m lane is enough and the platform always sits on the bus's left. Standard buses with left-side doors serve every platform.

The whole street, with walkways and scooter lanes on both sides, fits in 24 m. Corridors, which carry buses both ways, need about 90 ft.

About 110 stops in all, ~90 of them median stations with open platforms, shade roofs and arrival screens, each next to a scooter dock.

Risk and response

Keep cars out of the bus lane, and plan for narrow roads

Delhi's BRT was removed in 2016 partly because cars took over its lanes and enforcement was weak. Physical barriers and automatic fines from day one avoid that.

Car-trap pits at every entry

  • Pit wider than a car's track, narrower than a bus's: buses roll over, car wheels drop in
  • Only at low-speed entry points and stations, never along the lane
  • Bollards keep two-wheelers and autos out; red surface and signs before every pit
  • About ₹10–15 lakh each; ~100 pits, ₹10–15 crore. Road-safety audit before use

AI cameras, automatic fines

  • Number-plate cameras every 300–500 m, plus a forward camera on every bus (~₹50,000 each)
  • AI spots any vehicle in the lane, day and night
  • E-challan through Cyberabad Traffic Police; fines rise with each repeat
  • Tow truck called automatically for any stopped vehicle

The lane is bus-only all day; ambulances and fire engines may use it.

The walkway is never cut

It keeps at least 1.5 m on every stretch.

A car lane goes first

Before the bus lane or scooter lane is narrowed. Under metro pillars, the bus runs between pillars or at the kerb.

Land purchase is the last resort

Every stretch is measured in 2027 before design.

The street edge

Every bus road gets a walk-and-scoot edge

Each side: 1.5 m walkway, 2.0 m scooter lane, 0.5 m planted kerb, then two 3 m car lanes, 10 m in all. Because buses stop in the median, the kerb never has bus bays or waiting crowds, which is what makes a continuous scooter lane possible.

Five rules

1

No gaps

Walkway and lane cross gates and side streets at their own level.

2

Nothing in the walkway

Poles, stalls and signboards go in the planted kerb.

3

No stopping on the edge

A coloured kerb line, as in Tokyo.

4

A dock at every crossing

Beside each crossing to a median station.

5

Fenced corners

Set-back crossings, as in Tokyo and Dubai.

The standing e-scooter

A light, slow scooter for the last 3–5 km

The vehicle

Standing e-scooter, top speed 25 km/h, slowed to 10 km/h near stations by GPS; lights, bell and indicators.

Where it rides

Scooter lanes and slow residential streets, never walkways or bus-road car lanes; parked only in docks.

How people get one

Shared fleets from 2–3 licensed operators, or buy your own.

What the state must do

Notify a legal e-scooter category (standing scooters are not clearly covered today), license operators with fleet caps, riders 16 and over with helmets.

The Metro Bus

Board like Dubai: tap in, no conductor

In Dubai, one nol card works on metro, bus and tram, with a reader by every bus door and no conductor on board. Cyberabad’s Metro Bus works the same way.

One card, one tap

Metro card at a reader by every door. No cash, no tickets, no conductor.

₹10 a bus, ₹20 a journey

Any buses within 60 minutes; ₹40 daily cap, ₹900 monthly pass, same fare for all.

Minimum balance to board

As in Dubai, a card needs [₹20] to start a trip, so nobody rides on an empty card.

Clean, calm, watched

Electric and air-conditioned, CCTV on board, no eating or smoking, live arrival screens.

The fare

One tap, one simple fare

Same fare for every rider. Pay with the Hyderabad Metro card, a RuPay NCMC card or an app QR ticket; no cash on buses, no conductors.

Any number of buses within 60 minutes.

Why per bus, not per km

Buses are an open system: people get off onto open platforms and footpaths, not through gates as in the metro. Distance fares would need everyone to tap out inside the bus every time, and missed tap-outs mean overcharges and disputes. One tap when boarding, with a ₹20 journey cap, keeps transfers cheap, which the loop-and-corridor network depends on.

Checks and fallback

Roving inspectors with handheld readers; ₹500 penalty for riding without a tap. If long single-bus rides on C1 and C3 turn out to be common, a ₹15 corridor boarding fare is the fallback, decided on six months of tap data.

Costs

Rollout

Start with two loops, then grow

Phase 1 covers the densest office area — HITEC City, Gachibowli, Raidurg, Khajaguda and the Financial District. It opens with temporary kerbs and paint, then is built permanently. Phase 2 goes ahead only if Phase 1 proves demand.

The ask

Ten decisions to start in 2027

Approve the model, set the rules that make it work, and fund and organise the first phase.

Approve the model

1

The network: spine, loops and tributaries, starting with Metro Loops L1 and L2, then corridors C1, C2, C3 and loop L3, with about 110 Metro Nodes.

2

The street standard: a walk-and-scoot edge on every bus road, and a home-to-station route within 3–5 km of every node.

3

One Metro network, one card: metro, loops and corridors under one brand and map, with a joint metro and bus pass that keeps a worker’s month under ₹3,000.

4

The fare and contract: choose ₹10–20 or ₹15–30 a bus ride, and approve a per-km contract for electric, driver-only buses.

Set the rules

5

Bus-only median lanes enforced from day one, with car-trap pits, AI cameras and e-challans through Cyberabad Traffic Police.

6

Autos on the branches: zone permits, GPS tracking and dedicated parking at every node and neighbourhood, agreed with drivers’ unions.

7

A legal e-scooter category, notified by the Telangana Transport Department, with licensed shared-fleet operators and fleet caps.

Fund and organise

8

Fund Phase 1: about ₹470 crore for streets, opening with a ₹30 crore quick kit, and ₹115 crore for depots, stations and the fare system. Seek PM e-Bus Sewa support and depot land from TGIIC or HMDA.

9

One lead agency with GHMC, HMDA, TGIIC, TGSRTC, HMRL and Cyberabad Traffic Police on one programme board.

10

Start the 2027 work: a road-width survey of every route, a ridership survey with IT employers and an operator market check. Phase 2 goes ahead once Phase 1 carries 25,000 trips a day.
